# Motion grammar

Design motion in four independent channels:

1. **Formation** — how the visual world appears or transforms.
2. **Subject** — what the person, object or environment does.
3. **Camera** — how the viewer moves.
4. **Transition** — how visual state passes to the next shot.

Choose one dominant channel per beat. Use the other channels as support.

## Causal blueprint

Write actions as:

```text
trigger → material response → subject or camera response → lock or handoff
```

Prefer concrete verbs: seep, pool, drag, scratch, sweep, deposit, outline, lock, dissolve, absorb, fold, reveal. Avoid vague directions such as “make it cinematic.”

## Completion contract

For each shot define:

- Start state
- Trigger
- Dominant formation event
- Fixed or bounded subject motion
- Camera behavior
- Final composition
- Settle time or next-shot handoff
- Simplified fallback

The final state must emerge from the active layers or generated motion. Do not hide discontinuity by cutting to a different approved still.

## Ink-specific test

A convincing ink formation shows:

- an irregular water front rather than a smooth digital circle
- different travel speeds along paper fibres
- darker pigment at some wet edges
- local granulation or broken-brush gaps
- a later structural lock for important subjects
- a clear stop to uncontrolled diffusion

Use shader, Canvas, displacement, animated mattes, generated footage or a hybrid route as appropriate. The visual test matters more than the implementation label.

## Dynamic proof

Render the proof at low resolution and normal playback speed. Inspect both the video and frames around:

- first visible formation
- maximum diffusion or gesture
- subject lock
- transition or settling
- final held state

Do not approve solely from slow motion or still frames.
